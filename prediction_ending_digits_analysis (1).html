Do predictors round toward the top of the thousand?

Distribution of the last three digits of every user prediction, pooled across all tracked hunts. Testing the idea that guesses cluster high — like $xx,890 / $xx,960 — rather than low.

● Verdict — hypothesis not supported

The endings tilt slightly low, not high — and the real driver is round & "fun" numbers.

Across 20,045 predictions, the mean ending is 480.6 (a flat, random distribution would sit at 499.5), the median is 467, and 52.4% of endings fall in the lower half [000–499] versus 47.6% in the upper half [500–999] — the opposite direction from the guess, and statistically clear (z ≈ −6.8). The very top decile [900–999] is actually under-represented (6.3%, vs 10% if random). What genuinely dominates isn't "high," it's round: endings pile onto 000, multiples of 100/25, repeat-digits (555, 777, 333…), sequential runs (123, 456, 789…), and the meme value 420.

How this was built

Predictions were pooled from two sources in the tracker's data: the per-hunt live event logs (*_hunt_live_logs/hunt_*/predictions.jsonl) and the closing snapshots (*_pending_prediction_result_history.jsonl). Handling details:

  • Each user counts once per hunt — their final submitted amount. When a user revised their guess, only the latest value (by event timestamp / closing snapshot) is kept.
  • The bot's own automated entries (mine: true) are excluded, so this reflects organic predictors only.
  • Last-3-digits = integer part of the amount, mod 1000 (e.g. 22,543 → 543; 16,739.25 → 739). Amounts are whole numbers for 90% of entries; fractional values are truncated to the dollar before taking digits.
  • 34 hunts had at least one logged prediction (26 appear in both sources, 5 live-only, 3 snapshot-only); a handful of tracked hunts logged only events with no captured predictions and contribute nothing.
  • The distribution's departure from uniform is not subtle: a chi-square goodness-of-fit test against a flat 000–999 distribution gives χ² ≈ 71,400 (df = 999), i.e. essentially p ≈ 0. The endings are wildly non-random — just not in the high-skewing way hypothesized.
